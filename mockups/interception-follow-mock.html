MOCKUP — not the running app. Following a paused call through its whole cycle.

See the whole cycle, close it when you are done

Today a request breakpoint vanishes the moment you press Send, and you never see what came back. Below: the card stays, follows the call upstream, fills in the response, and waits for you to close it.

1 · Releasing a request — the new footer

The only new control is the Follow this call checkbox. Everything above it is exactly what ships today. The card staying on screen is not optional — that is the thing being asked for. The checkbox only decides whether Alfred stops again when the answer arrives, or just shows it to you.

2 · In flight — sent, nothing held

Between the two halves the card is not holding anybody: the caller's request is on its way to the supplier and no socket is waiting on you. So there is no countdown, the banner does not count it, and the tab badge does not either. What you already sent is on screen, read-only, with your edits marked.

4 · Finished — yours to keep or close

The caller has its answer and Alfred is holding nothing. The card stays until you close it, with the complete cycle and every edit you made to either half. Closing it is just dismissing a card — the call is long gone from the wire and is already in the call log.

A finished card is dropped automatically once there are more than 20 of them, oldest first, and every card is gone on a page reload — the registry is in memory on purpose.

What the counts mean now

Holding — a real client socket is open and waiting on you. This is the only number in the tab badge and the only one the banner shouts about. Following — sent upstream, nobody held, will stop again when it answers. Finished — nothing held at all, kept on screen purely for you to read. Mixing these into one number would make the badge cry wolf about calls nobody is waiting on.
